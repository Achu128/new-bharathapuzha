About Bharathapuzha Watershed

Understanding the lifeline of central Kerala

The Bharathapuzha, popularly known as Nila, is one of the most important rivers of Kerala. It is the second longest river in the state after the Periyar. The river originates in the Anamalai Hills of the Western Ghats and flows westwards through Tamil Nadu and Kerala before joining the Arabian Sea at Ponnani in Malappuram district.


The Bharathapuzha watershed is an important geographical region that collects rainfall and channels surface water into the main river and its tributaries. The basin extends across parts of Kerala and Tamil Nadu. In Kerala, it covers areas of Palakkad, Malappuram and Thrissur districts.


The river plays an important role in agriculture, irrigation, groundwater recharge, biodiversity and the cultural life of communities living along its banks. It is also closely associated with Kerala's history, literature and traditions.

Geographical Information

Physical and geographical characteristics

📍 Location

The watershed is situated in southern India, extending across Kerala and Tamil Nadu. It covers parts of Palakkad, Malappuram and Thrissur districts in Kerala.

⛰️ Origin

The river originates in the Anamalai Hills of the Western Ghats, an important mountain system of southern India.

🌊 Drainage

The river flows from east to west and drains into the Arabian Sea at Ponnani. Its drainage system includes several tributaries and streams.

🌧️ Climate

The watershed receives substantial rainfall during the Southwest Monsoon. Rainfall distribution varies between the Western Ghats, Palakkad region and coastal areas.

🌳 Physiography

The basin includes mountainous highlands, midland plateaus, valleys, agricultural plains and low-lying coastal regions.

🌱 Land Use

Major land-use categories include agricultural land, plantations, forests, settlements, river channels and water bodies.

Major Tributaries

Important rivers and streams of the basin

Kalpathipuzha

An important tributary associated with the Palakkad region. It is formed by the joining of smaller streams including Walayar, Malampuzha and Korayar.

Gayathripuzha

An important tributary originating in the Western Ghats region and draining areas around Nenmara and Alathur.

Chitturpuzha

Also known as Chittur River, it drains parts of the eastern Palakkad region.

Thuthapuzha

A major tributary that joins Bharathapuzha near Pallippuram and contributes significantly to the river's flow.

Major Dams of Bharathapuzha Watershed

Important reservoirs and irrigation projects associated with the basin

Malampuzha Dam

Malampuzha Dam is located near Palakkad town. It is constructed across the Malampuzha River, an important tributary of the Bharathapuzha.

The reservoir supports irrigation, drinking water supply and tourism. It is one of the major water resource projects in Palakkad.

Palakkad District

Kanjirapuzha Dam

Kanjirapuzha Dam is situated in the Mannarkkad region of Palakkad district. It is an important irrigation project associated with the Bharathapuzha basin.

The reservoir supports agricultural activities and water management in surrounding areas.

Palakkad District

Mangalam Dam

Mangalam Dam is located in the Alathur region of Palakkad district. It is constructed across the Cherukunnapuzha, a tributary of the Gayathripuzha system.

The reservoir plays an important role in irrigation and agricultural development.

Palakkad District

Meenkara Dam

Meenkara Dam is situated near Chittur in Palakkad district. It is constructed across the Meenkara River, which forms part of the Chitturpuzha drainage system.

It provides water for irrigation and supports agricultural activities in the region.

Palakkad District

Pothundi Dam

Pothundi Dam is located near Nenmara in Palakkad district. It is constructed across the Padippuzha and Meenchiladipuzha streams, tributaries of the Gayathripuzha system.

The reservoir is an important source of irrigation water for agricultural lands.

Palakkad District

Why Bharathapuzha matters

Supports agricultural activities, particularly paddy cultivation, through irrigation and water supply.

💧 Water Resources

Provides water for domestic purposes, irrigation and groundwater recharge.

🐟 Biodiversity

River channels and associated ecosystems support aquatic organisms and freshwater biodiversity.

🏛️ Cultural Heritage

The Nila River has an important place in Kerala's literature, history, festivals and cultural traditions.

Environmental Challenges and Conservation

Protecting the future of the Nila River

Major Environmental Challenges


1. Water Pollution: Domestic sewage, agricultural runoff and industrial waste can affect river water quality.


2. Sand Mining: Excessive sand extraction can disturb river channels, riverbanks and aquatic habitats.


3. Water Scarcity: Seasonal rainfall variation, increasing water demand and changes in river flow contribute to water stress.


4. Habitat Degradation: Changes in land use, deforestation and encroachment can damage river ecosystems.


Conservation Measures


• Promote sustainable water use and rainwater harvesting.

• Control illegal sand mining and riverbank encroachment.

• Improve sewage treatment and waste management.

• Protect wetlands and aquatic biodiversity.

• Encourage community participation in river conservation.
